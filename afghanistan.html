Afghanistan

Overview of Afghanistan's ongoing conflicts and security situation

Last Updated: March 28, 2026

🌍 Overview

Afghanistan continues to face insurgent activity, political instability, and a worsening humanitarian crisis. The Taliban maintains control over most of the country, but governance challenges persist due to economic strain and international isolation. ISIS-K remains the primary armed opposition, carrying out attacks targeting civilians, Taliban officials, and minority communities. Regional powers, including Pakistan, Iran, and China, continue to influence the security landscape.

📌 Ongoing Conflicts

  • Taliban consolidation of power continues, with reported internal factional tensions.
  • ISIS-K insurgency has intensified, including attacks in urban areas and against religious minorities.
  • Attacks on civilians, schools, and healthcare facilities persist, worsening humanitarian conditions.
  • Border tensions with Pakistan have escalated, including cross-border strikes and militant activity.
  • Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) insurgency spilling across the Afghanistan–Pakistan border.
  • Localized ethnic and tribal conflicts impacting governance and aid delivery.

🕊 Diplomacy & Regional Relations

  • Engagement with neighboring countries (Pakistan, Iran, China, and Central Asian states) continues for trade, border security, and political coordination.
  • Pakistan Relations: Relations with Pakistan have significantly deteriorated. Pakistan accuses the Taliban of harboring Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) militants responsible for attacks inside Pakistan. In response, Pakistan has conducted airstrikes and cross-border shelling on suspected militant positions inside Afghanistan, leading to civilian casualties and diplomatic tensions. The Taliban denies these allegations, increasing border instability along the Durand Line.
  • Border & Refugee Issues: Frequent clashes, border closures, and disputes over fencing continue along the Afghanistan–Pakistan border. Pakistan has also increased deportations of undocumented Afghan refugees, adding to humanitarian pressures.
  • UN and international NGOs continue to provide humanitarian aid, though funding shortages and restrictions limit effectiveness.
  • Regional powers influence Taliban policies and security priorities, with China showing growing economic interest in infrastructure and resources.
  • Attempts to stabilize governance and restore basic services continue amidst insecurity and limited international recognition.

🌍 Ongoing Military Operations

  • Taliban Security Operations: Ongoing counterinsurgency efforts against ISIS-K, particularly in eastern provinces such as Nangarhar and Kunar.
  • ISIS-K Attacks: Suicide bombings, ambushes, and targeted assassinations against Taliban officials, civilians, and minority groups.
  • Cross-Border Activity: Pakistani airstrikes and artillery responses targeting suspected militant positions along the border.

What You Need to Know:

  • The Taliban remains the de facto authority but faces persistent threats from ISIS-K.
  • Urban attacks have increased, raising concerns about long-term stability.
  • Pakistan–Afghanistan tensions are one of the most volatile aspects of the conflict.
  • The humanitarian crisis continues to worsen, with widespread food insecurity and limited healthcare access.
  • International focus remains on preventing terrorism spillover and sustaining humanitarian aid.
